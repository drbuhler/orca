The framing is done — whew .

I've been waiting a while to write that sentence.


## Scaffolds down

We pulled some of the scaffolding this week and got our first real look at the house in months.

That massive room is finally readable as a room. And the new windows — they are big. Bigger in person than they ever looked on the plans. Standing in there with the light coming through is the first time this project has felt like a house again.

## Shear walls next week
Now that the framing is up, we sheathe it. 

Shear walls are the thin layer of wood we add over the framing, and they're what keeps the house from racking sideways in an earthquake or a big coastal wind.

It's a quiet week of work that doesn't quiet 'wow' visually but adds the structural integrity we need. 

After that, the shell is dry.

## Final Window approval

Our structural engineer came out to walk the site and signed off on some modifications to the window design.

Worth explaining why that matters: every opening you put in a wall is wall you don't have anymore, structurally speaking. The engineer has to check that what's left still carries the load. So window changes aren't just a design decision — they get calculated, reviewed, and approved before anyone builds them.

Ours are approved. The big glass stays.
